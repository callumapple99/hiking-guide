Our Key Criteria

So what do we think is valuable information to know before setting off?

There's a wealth of questions you might ask yourself before leaving the house, so we've compiled a list of the most important information that we think you should know before setting off on your next adventure!

Remember that both our criteria and the world are constantly evolving. If you've come here looking for information that we don't have please let us know, we welcome suggestions that improve the quality of our site and makes hiking more accessible to everyone.

Alternatively if you get out and about only to find that one of our reviews is out of date then leave your own to keep our site as accurate as possible and to help the next person looking for information. Remember roads close, bathrooms get renovated and trail conditions can deteriorate, so if you'd want to know about this beforehand then so will someone else - leave a hike review and keep your fellow hikers as up to date as you.

As a last note you might find a lot of questions to ask or get answered below. No one expects you to spend your well earned post-hike euphoria writing a full debrief of your experiences, but if you benefitted from the guidance on this site (or you think someone else can benefit from yours!) then whatever information you can share will be greatly appreciated.

The Journey Before the Journey

What was the starting point of your hike and how did you find getting there? Was the road condition good? Should a nervous driver be aware that the roads were single track, or did you find the directions difficult to follow at any point? Did you take public transport, drive or teleport to your destination?

Any information you can provide will be certain to help the next awkward hiker!

Note: While detailed information is helpful to everyone we ask that you ensure you don't leave any information that could lead back to your home.
If you drove then leave guidance from the closest motorway or major road junction you took, or from the city/town you started at.

Parking

How easy did you find parking?

Was the car park large or small? Did you have to pay to park - if so was it by card or cash, was exact change required?

Would you recommend the car park or would you suggest finding a different site or starting point?

Was the car park close to the beginning of the trail you took?

As part of your profile we'll ask how experienced a driver you are and tag it against this section of your review - remember if you've been driving for 30 years your experience may differ greatly from a beginner driver or someone not used to navigating countryside roads. This experience level logic is one of the key fundamentals that spawned The Awkward Hiker.

Bathrooms and other amenities

Perhaps the most important question we'll ask - was there a bathroom?

Every experienced hiker knows the pain of deciding you need to use the bathroom, only to be miles from the closest one. So let us know about your route, was there a bathroom? Was it usable? Did you have to pay, and was it public or part of a shop/cafe/pub?

Of course bathrooms aren't the only facilities you might want to see on your hike, so on your visit did you find any other interesting or useful local amenities?

How busy is the route?

Some people love a social hike, others prefer their own company. Where there lots of people around during your journey?

There's another very important reason to ask this question - safety. If you had a fall, would someone find you?

On a well-travelled route it's a reasonable bet that should you find yourself in trouble, someone will be close by to help. Whereas on a wilder path you may need to take more measures to ensure your own safety.

Remember: Never leave your safety to another person. Research your route and bring suitable equipment. Ensure people know exactly where you've gone, and when to raise the alarm if you've not checked in. Never take any unnecesary risks.

Is there phone signal?

Perhaps the most obvious question we'll ask. How was the phone signal?

While we always recommend downloading your route in advance (and ideally having a backup paper map and compass option!) it's nice to know if you'll be able to phone home or check the football score on route.

Route Condition and Difficulty

There's a variety of grading systems for hiking and climbing but these can be daunting for a hiker inexperienced with what they actually entail and what the difference between a grade 2 or a grade 3 means. At times they can also be somewhat subjective.

So tell us how you found the route. Was the route in good condition? Did you come across any sections that were muddy, overgrown, contained loose gravel or steep rock faces? Did you come across any difficult to pass sections? Did you pass any obstacles like rivers, stiles or sections that required scrambling (using your hands and knees!).

As we like to continuously state, everyone's experience is different - so we'll tag your experience of the hike with your rough hiking experience level to let other hikers find reviews that are most suitable for them.

Route Ease to Follow and Right to Roam

Was the route easy to follow or did you take any wrong turns? Did you have to take any diversions? Was the path well-travelled and well signposted or did you find yourself regularly consulting the map?

Lots of hikers also appreciate advanced notice of whose land you're walking on. While both legal and completely normal, for some people the right to roam that allows the public to use farmers' land can make them a little nervous. Did you find yourself in any fields full of livestock, farmers' gardens or walking through any farmland?

Other Criteria

All the above are what we find most important before setting off on your journey, but there's a million more things you might want to know.

Did you encounter any wildlife?

Deer? Birds? Any interesting plant life? Don't just tell us, show us!

Did you visit any of the local pubs, cafes or shops? How about equipment hire for bikes, canoes, hiking gear or anything else you can imagine?

Let your fellow hikers know where the best places to visit are.

Are there any worthwhile detours on your route?

Did you take any detours to see anything interesting? Caves, landmarks or incredible views, side quests are what makes hiking the best hobby in the world!
